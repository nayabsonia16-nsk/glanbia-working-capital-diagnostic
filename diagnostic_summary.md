Glanbia plc: Working Capital Diagnostic
A self-directed analysis of Glanbia's operational cash efficiency, 2023–2025

Problem
Is Glanbia's working capital efficiency improving or deteriorating, and where is the cash opportunity?

Method
Built a SQL database (MySQL) from Glanbia's published annual reports (2023–2025), calculated four core working capital KPIs (Inventory Turnover, DSO, DPO, Cash Conversion Cycle), benchmarked against sector peer Kerry Group, modelled improvement scenarios, and visualised findings in Power BI.

Key Findings

Glanbia's Cash Conversion Cycle worsened from 24.5 days (2023) to 37.4 days (2025), driven mainly by a sharp rise in Inventory Days and DPO in 2024 — coinciding with Glanbia's divestment of part of its business that year
Benchmarked against Kerry Group, Glanbia collects payment from customers roughly twice as fast (DSO ~2x lower) — indicating the deterioration is Glanbia-specific rather than an industry-wide trend
Returning DSO and Inventory Days to their own 2023 levels would free up an estimated €404.7m in cash — a realistic target since Glanbia has already demonstrated this level of efficiency before

Recommendation
Prioritise inventory and receivables management as the primary levers for cash release, rather than extending supplier payment terms further, since DPO is already elevated relative to 2023.

Data & Methodology Notes

2023 comparative figures were sourced from the 2024 annual report (not the standalone 2023 report) to ensure consistency, since Glanbia restated results following a 2024 portfolio divestment
A data entry error (using pre-exceptional rather than total 2025 COGS) was identified and corrected during the build
Kerry Group's Cost of Goods Sold was not directly disclosed; "Raw Materials and Consumables" was used as the closest available proxy. As a result, only DSO was used for cross-company benchmarking, since Inventory Days and DPO comparisons would be distorted by this differing cost-base definition
Kerry Group's 2023 Raw Materials figure was unavailable due to a reporting format change; this year was excluded from COGS-dependent comparisons rather than estimated.
